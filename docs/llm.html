The victim model

  • We train our own 11M model from random init, single-pass over 98M tokens of SimpleStories, sweeping the learning rate (1e-03, 3e-03, 6e-03, 1e-02) and keeping the best (1e-03). This is the "victim" whose weights get stolen.
  • We deliberately keep it undertrained — single-pass, loss still descending — because that is the realistic regime for token-limited frontier models (no multi-epoch memorisation).
  • Target for recovery = this victim's eval loss (1.9335); denominator = its from-scratch step count (3000 steps). Recovery cost is reported as a % of that.
  • We compress all 43 2D weight matrices (44 MB fp32: attention + MLP + tied embedding); RMSNorm scales are tiny and kept dense.

Eval loss vs. step for each LR; the lowest (1e-3) becomes the victim. The curve is still sloping down at the end — the model is undertrained by construction.

How does this compare to the published model?

We evaluated the released SimpleStories-11M on our exact held-out set (same tokenizer and metric), and read the suite's training recipe from the paper (arXiv:2504.09184, Sec 3.5) and its training config.

Our victim sees ~20× fewer tokens (single-pass vs ~6 epochs) and lands ~17% higher perplexity — undertrained, but a coherent story model. That gap is the point: a token-limited, not-fully-converged model is the realistic target for weight theft.
